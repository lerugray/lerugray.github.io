Whenever I post about my wargames on Facebook, or someone else does, the same handful of wargaming influencers shows up in the comments. They call the work garbage and call me a plagiarist. I haven't spoken to the man who leads this in more than ten years. Mostly I let it go. I'm writing this once so I can link to it instead of answering it again.

The accusation with the most behind it is about Mars la Tour. Its system is built on Mark Herman's Gettysburg, and I asked him first. On August 31, 2019, I messaged him on BoardGameGeek: I was researching a Gettysburg-inspired game, I wanted to know if he was okay with it, and I offered to credit him and share royalties. He wrote back "Go for it," and asked me to send him a copy when it came out. I published a screenshot of that exchange in 2022, and here it is again.

Where Mars la Tour's rules work the same way as his, I kept his wording in the manual. He's the premier designer in the hobby, and I didn't see the point in trying to explain his mechanics better than he already had. Those paragraphs are marked in the rulebook as paraphrased from him. The rules credit him at the top of the first page and again in the credits, the map says "System Design by Mark Herman," and the store page calls it his original design. He knew the game was built on his, and he'd said yes in writing. The rulebook is a free download on the [CSL site](https://www.consimsltd.com/downloads) if you want to check.

The other accusation is that DAMOS rips off GDW's 120 series game 1941, because the boxes look alike. They're small boxes and both play in about two hours. That's where it ends. 1941 has no reaction movement, no battle plans and no variable combat dice, and DAMOS is built on all three. The DAMOS manuals are free on the same downloads page.

I've named where my systems come from since I started. Imperial Bayonets builds on Kevin Zucker's Library of Napoleonic Battles, with his permission. The World Undone owes a lot to SPI's The Marne, and Sedan 1940 owes John Tiller's Panzer Campaigns. That's normal. Unless your name is Volko Ruhnke or Mark Herman, you probably aren't inventing new mechanics. You're using what wargame designers have borrowed from each other for decades: zones of control, line of sight, supply, combat results tables. Read the stealing rule strictly and every chit-pull game is stealing from the first one, which makes the whole charge kind of hilarious.

There are real criticisms of my work, and none of these people make them. My BoardGameGeek page makes the case better than they do. I've released broken games. I've discontinued games that shouldn't have gone out in the first place. I overcomplicate things, and early on I took on topics before I'd studied them long enough. I have three Charles S. Roberts nominations, and I'd still put myself somewhere between mediocre and above average as a designer. I came to the hobby as an amateur and an outsider, and I stayed mostly out of stubbornness, because I wanted games on Sedan 1870 and the 2nd Risorgimento and nobody else was going to make them.

Any of that would be a fair hit. What I get instead follows my politics. I'm a vocal anti-Zionist Jew, and my politics border on communist. A lot of the wargaming influencer scene won't tolerate that, and I think that's what this is about. My politics have nothing to do with how a combat results table works.

I've thought about hiring a lawyer. I don't have the money, and I don't want to spend my life on what is basically high school. So this is the whole response. If you want to trash my work, go ahead; some of it has earned that. At least have the decency to go after the games.
