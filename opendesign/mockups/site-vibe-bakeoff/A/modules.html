I build digital modules of print wargames on commission. The map, the counters, and the procedures are translated into a local engine. You unzip a folder and double-click index.html. It runs in your browser. No installs, no accounts, no internet connection required. If you have no one to play, the module can play the other side.

What a module includes

Automated bookkeeping and chart lookups. Hotseat play with information masking for cards and hidden movement. Every roll logs the cited rule. Games save to local files. The price includes a computer opponent that can take either side.

From a client

Bloody April

Bloody April is GMT's game of the 1917 air war over Arras: flights of aircraft at different altitudes, sighting rules, and movement unlike a standard hex-and-counter game. David Greenwood commissioned the module and a computer opponent that plays either side.

I highly recommend Ray's work at developing AI opponents for wargames. I presented him with the challenge of Bloody April, a WWI operational air war game. This game is quite complex, and while it is a hex and counter game, it is very different from standard hex and counter games. It includes multilevel flight of groups of planes, sighting rules and unusual movement rules. In order to create an AI, Ray had to first digitize the game. He took it on enthusiastically, and the result is I can now play a favorite game that I have had trouble finding human opponents for. The AI is a challenging opponent and can play either side.

Working with Ray has been great. He gave frequent updates, was open to suggestions and requests, and was very accommodating. He developed an innovative approach to testing, using a panel of AI developers to simulate games and then rate the results. This enabled him to go through cycles of development and testing in a very short amount of time.

This was a VERY complex project due to its atypical rules. If someone wants a standard SPI-like or other standard hex and counter ground wargame, he can complete it within days. Either way, given what it would cost to have a developer do similar work, it is a bargain.

David Greenwood, Bloody April commission

Terms

I do not build these to undercut designers or publishers. I build them so players who struggle to find an opponent get a competent, reliable one, and so I can add a little to my minimum-wage income. I am a designer and publisher myself, and I listen when one of them raises a concern.

Anyone with the game and an LLM harness could build one of these today. I sell my experience and labor in building them. If a designer or publisher asks me in writing not to adapt one of their titles, I stop taking commissions for it.

  1. Personal use only — A commission is a private build for you. Do not share, resell or post the module files. If a build turns up in public, support for it ends and I help the publisher get it taken down.
  2. You must own the game — You need a print copy of the edition you name, and you show it to me before I start. Placing an order confirms you own it. The module lets you play the copy you already own.
  3. Not for games that already have a digital edition with a computer opponent — If one exists, buy that. I decline those commissions.
  4. Designers and publishers can opt out — If you designed or published a game and do not want it adapted, email rweiss@consimsltd.com. I add the title to the list below the day your email arrives, decline every commission for it from then on, and answer you promptly.
